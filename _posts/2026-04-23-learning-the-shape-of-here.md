I've been learning to live in the present tense.

Not in the way productivity articles mean it — not optimized presence, not mindfulness as performance — but the slower, less glamorous version. The one where you sit with the actual texture of your days and notice what's there.

<!--more-->

What I've found is that I know more about myself than I thought I did, and less than I assumed. Both of those things feel true at the same time, which is either a contradiction or just what honesty looks like.

I know I need depth. In conversation, in work, in how I spend my attention. I've always been this way, but I see it more clearly now. Shallow water has its own kind of loneliness — you can be surrounded by people and still feel it. The connections I've valued most have always been the ones where you get below the surface quickly and find out there's actually something there.

I know I'm better at sitting with uncertainty than I used to be. That's not something I trained for; it's something that happened to me. There's a version of the Buddhist concept of *shoshin* — beginner's mind — that I keep returning to: approaching the situation you're in without the conclusions you already wrote. Letting things be a little unresolved. Noticing what that opens up instead of rushing to close it.

What I'm less sure about is harder to name.

Something in me is orienting. Like a compass settling after being held near a magnet. I'm not certain where it's pointing yet, and I've made a kind of peace with that — which is itself new. The question might not be *where* anyway. It might be what kind of terrain I want to be moving through. What kind of company I want to keep.

The philosopher Gaston Bachelard wrote about how we learn our own contours by understanding what holds us and what doesn't — that inhabiting a space teaches you something about your own shape. I've been thinking about that. About what I've learned from the holding, and from the places I didn't fit.

What I can say as plainly as I know how: I'm here. Not performing okayness, not projecting some particular version of forward motion — just genuinely present in my own life in a way that feels more honest than it has in a while. Some days that's quiet and a little strange. Other days it has an unexpected lightness to it.

I don't know what comes next. I'm finding I'm more curious about that than I expected to be.

That feels like something worth paying attention to.
